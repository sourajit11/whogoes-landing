1Change which room you're in, not the filter

Your site says Causum works with regulated industries and starts by asking what a decision in that domain has to prove. The people who ask that question don't go to AI conferences. They go to their own industry's events.

Where you've been looking

AI events

Founders, researchers and AI vendors. Everyone has their own model to talk about, so they pitch rather than listen.

→

Where Causum's buyers are

Finance, insurance and healthcare events

Chief AI Officers, CROs, heads of model risk, AI governance leads and regulators. They have AI in production and need to defend every decision it makes.

2Who we looked for

We filtered by the type of company first and the person's role at the event second. So a bank's Chief AI Officer counts whether they're attending, speaking or staffing their bank's booth.

Decision owners

Chief AI, Data and Risk Officers. Heads of AI governance, model risk and compliance at banks, insurers, health systems, payers and pharma companies.

Regulators

AI and innovation teams at MAS, the FCA, CSSF and BaFin. A proof that anyone can check is the language they speak.

People who can introduce you

Reps and managers at buyer companies who are staffing a booth. They're easy to meet and can walk you to the right person inside.

7How to use this before HLTH and SFF

Speakers get a lot of messages during the event week. The meetings come from reaching out before it.

This week

Engage on their post

Every person here posted about the event. Comment on that post with something useful about their session or their company's AI work.

3 to 5 days before

Connect and email

Connect on LinkedIn, then send a short email suggesting a 15-minute coffee at the event. Tie it to what a decision in their domain has to prove.

At the event

Use the booths

Visit the buyer booths. Ask the rep who owns AI risk or governance, and get a warm introduction to that person.
